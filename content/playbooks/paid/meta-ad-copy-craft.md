Copy is part of the retrieval signal, not decoration. The standard is not more variants; it is meaningfully different concepts a human can recognise and a machine can route.

Four lenses, in order: product truth (what is factually true and compliant) → platform truth (what the platform rewards and restricts right now) → brand voice (how this client sounds) → craft (scroll-stop, hook, density, slot fit). Produce three variants per slot only after the concept portfolio is named; score on hook strength, brand fit, compliance, specificity and concept distinction; ship the winner.

Hooks — the first three words of primary text carry most of the scroll-stop: pain-led ("Most X wreck Y."), curiosity gap ("Most X is the wrong kind."), contrarian, personal ("I tried four in two years."), specific result, numbered proof, comparison, relief-led, object-led ("Black leather. Clean lines."), setting-led ("First tee, early."). Never: questions implying diagnosis, "Discover / Introducing", superlatives ("amazing", "game-changing"), "Hey guys", emoji fireworks.

Headlines: under about 40 characters, benefit word first, no question marks, a concrete noun or number early, one idea, full sentences end with a period.

Primary text: everything critical above the fold (about 125 characters). Six patterns — problem/agitate/solve, before/after/bridge, hook/pivot/payoff, customer-voice lead, object/world/action, proof/relief/CTA.

Premium and luxury: not louder, more exact. Named materials, named settings, earned proof, emotional utility, short declarative sentences. Avoid "elevate", "unmatched", "ultimate", fake scarcity.

Compliance: every claim traceable to a source; no personal-attribute language, no before/after bodies, no cure or treat claims, no unverifiable scale claims; category overlays (health, finance) applied before launch.

QA before live: hook in first three words, headline under 40, above-fold value landed, one CTA matching the button, quotes verbatim and real, three variants scored.
