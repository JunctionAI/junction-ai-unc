Google Ads for an expensive product with thin conversion volume is a visibility and patience game, not a scaling game. Automation is data-hungry; below roughly thirty conversions a month it guesses.

The method:
1. Never manage from the UI labels. "Low / Good / Best" is not data. Extract per-asset-group, per-search-term and per-product numbers (scripts or the warehouse) before any decision.
2. Bid strategy follows conversion volume, not ambition. Target ROAS only when order values vary AND volume clears the learning bar; target CPA for flat values; below the bar, max-clicks with strict CPC caps or manual on exact-match cores until volume earns automation.
3. Intent-tier structure: a brand campaign (protect the name — cheap, always-on, exact brand terms, capped CPCs, high impression-share target); an exact/phrase non-brand core of the handful of terms that actually mean "buying this"; Shopping or Performance Max for breadth only with an approved, healthy feed. Small budgets die when spread thin — concentrate.
4. Negatives as automation, not hygiene sprints: continuous mining of search terms with spend and zero conversions.
5. Decision windows sized to volume: at one to three conversions a day-equivalent a "bad week" is noise. Kill or keep on three-to-four-week windows with spend-weighted comparisons.
6. The progression: check (extract) → chart (see) → change (manual, evidenced) → control (automation within guardrails). Autonomy is earned by instrumentation.

On/off rule shape: pause when four-week spend exceeds about three times target CPA with zero conversions AND search-term quality confirms mis-intent — both conditions, never spend alone.

Failure modes: smart bidding on thin volume; Performance Max before the feed is healthy (it quietly becomes a display network); single-week reactions; value bidding fed by broken conversion tracking; brand cannibalisation counted as PMax success.
