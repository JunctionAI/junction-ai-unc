Creative is the lever. Test concepts, not variants: each ad must give the buyer and the platform a genuinely different reason to care — product or use case, place or context, proof type, creator or person, problem, aspiration, offer or occasion. Ten near-identical variants are noise; four to eight meaningfully different concepts are inventory for the retrieval system.

Test order: hooks → formats → angles → offers. Hold body and call to action constant and test three to five hooks against the same skeleton.

Kill and keep on spend allocation, not ad-level ROAS. The platform starves losers on its own; an ad with poor individual ROAS may still be supporting the campaign. Pause what gets no spend; don't execute ROAS-sorted kill lists.

Fatigue is led, not lagged: first-time impression rate falling, frequency rising, click-through sliding — act on these before cost per acquisition spikes. Retire creative when frequency runs high and click-through drops.

Scale trigger: a winner identified by sustained spend allocation and stable seven-day-click ROAS at or above target across two windows → raise budget by about twenty percent or duplicate into value optimisation.

Volume: a steady stream of new concepts every week is the difference between a ceiling and a curve. Mix UGC-style video, clean product statics, founder story, social proof overlays and education.

Quality gate: measure creative ops by first-pass approval rate — how often creative ships without re-edits. Volume without a quality gate is noise; a gate without volume can't feed the machine. In the product, the founder's approval is that gate.

For premium brands: the ad must look expensive — polish is a targeting parameter. Discount-led hooks buy cheap conversions and train the algorithm toward bargain-hunters.
