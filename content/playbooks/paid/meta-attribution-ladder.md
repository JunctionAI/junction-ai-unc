Platform-reported ROAS is almost always inflated by its attribution window, and on thin volume it whipsaws weekly. Three layers, three jobs, never mixed:

1. Optimise on seven-day click (one-day-click bid caps where used). This is what the platform learns against.
2. Evaluate on 28-day click — the closest public proxy to incremental for most DTC accounts.
3. Steer the business on blended MER (total revenue ÷ total ad spend) over multi-week windows. Only this is decision-grade.

Ground truth is the store's own orders. Track platform-claimed revenue AND store-verified revenue; the gap is the platform inflation factor and it belongs in every report. Report the conservative number.

Practical rules:
- Never move budget on a single week of platform ROAS on a small account.
- Label every platform figure as a claim; label a missing figure as unmeasured, never estimated.
- Before trusting any channel's numbers, confirm the conversion event actually arrives at the destination (see the measurement truth audit): a tag that is "installed" is not evidence.

How Unc talks about it: when the founder asks "what's my ROAS", the honest answer names which layer the number comes from and which store-verified figure it reconciles to — or says plainly that the channel isn't connected or isn't measured yet.
