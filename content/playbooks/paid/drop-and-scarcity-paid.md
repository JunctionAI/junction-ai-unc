When inventory is finite, the inventory rules outrank every other optimisation. The single most expensive mistake is treating a sell-out as a success and leaving the ad running — every impression after the last unit is waste and trains the pixel on failed sessions.

Inventory-first rules:
1. Never launch against a SKU whose stock can't absorb the spend. Runway = units ÷ (daily budget ÷ allowable CPA). Under about ten days, don't launch that SKU — point at a collection or a deeper variant.
2. Point ads at a collection wherever the range allows, so one variant selling out kills one ad, not the campaign.
3. Stock-check before every scale recommendation. A strong ROAS on three remaining units is a signal to stop, not scale.
4. Automate the pause: stock at zero pauses the ad the same day.
5. Restocks beat new SKUs for paid — the creative is proven and the audience already wanted it.

The drop arc: tease (reach the community with the object, not an offer) → capture (waitlist or notify-me — the highest-leverage step and the most skipped) → drop (retarget the waitlist first, then widen) → sell-through (retarget viewers who didn't buy) → sold out (pause everything for that SKU, convert residual demand into the next waitlist). The waitlist is the compounding asset.

Creative: use the founder's organic that already proved itself in the feed; gate on engagement rate, not reach; collabs import another community's trust; the object is the ad. Scarcity must be true.

Targeting: broad plus creative-as-targeting for prospecting; the follower audience is the best retargeting seed but expect the matched audience to be far smaller than the follower count; retarget with early access, not discounts.

Measure sell-through speed and waitlist growth alongside ROAS; blended, not platform-reported; repeat-purchase rate is the real score.
