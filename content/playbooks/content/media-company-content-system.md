Stop thinking of the business as a company that also posts and start operating as a media company that happens to sell a product.

Three structural moves:
1. People, not logos. The company page is a weak vehicle. Recurring, recognisable characters (the founder, the team, collaborators) build their own audiences alongside the business account. A solo founder gets the "people not logos" half; the de-risking half (reach not attached to one person) needs a cast — name that gap honestly rather than fake it.
2. Four jobs, four categories. Entertainment (reach), inspiration (trust), education (authority), product (conversion). File every post to one job before it goes out and judge it by that job's number — never kill a trust post for low reach. Treat the mapping as a planning heuristic, not a forecast; it is asserted, not measured.
3. Format beats topic. The unit of research is the format, not the idea. Research formats well past the point of boredom, double down on the ones that work, then pour topic after topic through the winning format. Name the format, count how many times it has run, know its median result.

The harvest rule: a high-effort shoot is never used once. Before filming, attach a harvest list — original cut, reaction cut, behind-the-scenes hook, second-angle opening, long-form parent, text derivation. Re-cut genuinely (new opening, new first two seconds), not re-upload.

Distribution: use the surfaces that reach non-followers for testing (trial-style posts), then upgrade the winner. They are a testing instrument, not a growth lever.

Monthly audit questions: which job was each post for, and am I judging it by that job's number? Which format is winning, and what's the next topic to pour through it?
