Don't write — assemble. Great copy is found, not invented.

1. Mine voice of customer first: the exact phrases real buyers use from reviews, sales calls, surveys ("what was going on in your world that brought you here today?"), support chats. Research on one screen, the draft on the other; highlight the lines that make you pay attention and revise them into copy. The blank page is the mistake.
2. Draft from their words in a deliberate message order: the headline earns the subhead, the subhead earns the scroll. Wireframe the copy before design; copy leads, design serves.
3. Run every headline and claim through the three-rule gate: Visual (can I picture it? "1,000 songs in your pocket", not "large capacity") · Falsifiable (could a competitor honestly claim the opposite? if not, it's filler) · Unique (swap in a rival's logo — does the line still fit? then cut it).
4. "So what?" every feature down to the outcome: we do X → which means you can Y → so that you finally Z. Ship the Z.
5. Edit hard: more periods, fewer commas; kill adjectives and adverbs; write how you talk. Ban the landing-page words: unlock, unleash, enhance, exceed, empower, supercharge, seamless, revolutionary, cutting-edge, best-in-class, next-level, game-changing.
6. Tension over pleasant — name the enemy or the painful status quo; pleasant gets forgotten.

Quality bar: every claim passes all three rules; the main value proposition traces to a real customer phrase; zero landing-page words; reads aloud like a person; a competitor could not paste your hero onto their site.
