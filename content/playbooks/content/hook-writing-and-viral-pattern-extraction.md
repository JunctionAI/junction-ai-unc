Mine what's winning, decouple the mechanic from the topic, and hand back a fill-in-the-blank skeleton the product can be poured into.

The process:
1. Pull winners by primary metrics only (spend, results, cost per result) — these say which won, nothing else.
2. Decode the content, not the numbers, across five dimensions: format, creator, messaging, imagery, persona. This is where the transferable pattern lives.
3. Classify the messaging angle into a bucket — human desires, demographics, strategy angles (contrarian, taboo, personal history), user-journey stage. Name the angle, not the product; this is the topic-decoupling step.
4. Read the retention curve to find the load-bearing mechanic: hook rate (the first two or three seconds) and hold rate (three to fifteen seconds). Where people stayed is the format mechanic; where they dropped is what to cut. A great hook with weak hold looks viral but doesn't transfer.
5. Compare across sources — a pattern that recurs across brands is a format, not a fluke.
6. Extract the skeleton with the topic stripped: hook mechanic → angle bucket → body pacing → payoff → call to action, mapped to a named persona.
7. Pour the product in and isolate one variable: hold body and CTA constant, test three to five hooks.

Hooks are a psychology problem, not a template lookup: a curiosity gap or a conflicting statement keeps the brain in to resolve it. Study earned-click platforms (YouTube titles and thumbnails) for hook craft, not just ad libraries.

Failure modes: stopping at the dashboard; over-indexing on format (angle, creator and persona transfer more); iteration paralysis that converges all creative into sameness; changing several variables at once; full AI avatars audiences punish.

How Unc runs it: Viral hook mining does steps one to six on the niche each week and proposes three to five hooks per skeleton; the Founder content engine drafts around the founder's real footage; the founder approves hooks before anything is produced or posted.
