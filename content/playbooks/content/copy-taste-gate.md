The last pass on every piece of copy before it ships. Three gates in order; it fails closed.

1. Voice — before drafting, get the subject's real language on the page (reviews, founder transcript, existing on-brand copy) and draft from that. A technically clean paragraph in the wrong voice is a fail, not a near-miss.
2. Craft — open on something real (an object, a moment, a number) and let the claim arrive once it's earned, never paragraph one. Specific-and-true beats abstract-and-flattering: reveal the real spec even when a vaguer claim sounds safer. Falsifiable or cut. Vary sentence length so it has a pulse; read it aloud. Cut until removing a word changes the meaning. Concede a real limit where the topic warrants it — honesty reads as taste and as premium.
3. Slop — strip the statistical tells of machine writing. Banned words: delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, vibrant, leverage, foster, pivotal, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, unleash, supercharge, realm, navigate (figurative), underscore, resonate, myriad, bustling, game-changing, cutting-edge, revolutionary, best-in-class, effortless. Banned openers and shapes: "In today's world", "It's worth noting", "It's not just X, it's Y", "At the end of the day", "Whether you're a… or a…", rule-of-three as a reflex, uniform sentence length, an em-dash in every paragraph, emoji as bullets, any stat you can't source.

Failure modes: slop-stripping into sterility (voice and craft must run first); the banlist as dogma (a deliberate banned word in a real voice can be right — a human call overrides); voice drift in the middle of a long piece; faux-honesty where the concession is trivial.

Junction's own voice runs through this gate too: first person, numbers over adjectives, propose and show the working, no hype, no exclamation marks.
