Dead stock costs storage every month and starves cash flow, so the objective is cash recovered per week — but the brand outlives the clearance, so discounting gets last-resort status.

The mechanism set, in the order to use it:
1. Bonus spend — give the slow item away profitably: "free X on orders over a threshold" set above current AOV plus the item's cash value. Clears stock, raises AOV, zero price damage.
2. Bundles — slow plus fast at a price that beats separates but protects blended margin. Name the bundle like a product, not a deal.
3. Threshold ladders — free shipping or gift tiers just above current AOV; move the tier as AOV moves.
4. Staged public markdown, last — 10 → 20 → 30 on a declared timeline per stock tier, one direction only (a price that goes back up kills trust in every future markdown), heroes excluded, the brand's own framing ("we made too much" not "clearance").
5. Private offers — deeper cuts only in owned channels with codes; the public price stays intact. Gated by recency/frequency/value: at-risk and lapsed get depth, champions get early access instead of discounts.
6. Urgency that's true — unit counters and windows only with real numbers.
7. Exit valve — whatever survives goes to bulk or liquidation; recovering a little beats paying storage on zombies. Founder's call, with the maths.

Operating rules: the scoreboard is units cleared and cash recovered per week; return on ad spend stays a guardrail (per-product breakevens), not the goal. Full-price mechanics get two to four weeks of real effort before any public markdown. Email carries the deepest offers privately at near-zero acquisition cost; ads clear at target return and never show a public discount before the markdown phase is declared. Never spend a discount on someone who was buying anyway.

Daily ads ops during clearance: kill (below breakeven at adequate spend), hold (thin data), scale (a winner with stock runway) — proposed daily, executed on approval; sold out means off the same day.
