Plan against the customer journey, not the channels. Channels are delivery; the journey is the structure.

Tag every campaign to a stage: Reach (awareness with people who don't know the brand), Act (a first interaction), Convert (the purchase), Engage (repeat, loyalty, advocacy). A healthy quarter covers all four; if most campaigns are Convert, the plan is a discount calendar wearing a strategy costume.

Every campaign is one of three types, and the mix is the cadence: Hero (a few tentpoles a quarter — full cross-channel push, build-up weeks, real budget), Hub (regular mid-tier pushes, roughly monthly, one or two channels), Help (always-on — evergreen content, SEO, lifecycle flows, retargeting; never off). Roughly two to three Hero and six to nine Hub per quarter with Help underneath.

Flight, don't dump: lay the tentpole spine first (seasonal peaks, industry dates, the client's own key periods, known launches); every Hero starts one to three weeks before its date and follows through after; keep Hub and Help live between Heroes; never two heavy promotions back to back — put an Engage or Reach between Converts.

Ideas go into a persistent backlog that ranks itself. Score with RICE (reach × impact × confidence ÷ effort) or ICE for small bets; triage with must/should/could/won't against the declared goals. A high score on something orphaned from strategy is still a "won't". Pick four or five top-ranked ideas a quarter plus one or two lower-scored bets you genuinely believe in.

Capacity realism: an empty week is not free capacity. Check the live workload and the founder's hours before placing anything; a Hero needs its build weeks free, not just the launch week.

The living loop, every planning run: reconcile (what shipped, slipped, was cancelled) → re-anchor (does each campaign still serve a current goal? kill orphans) → capture and score new ideas → re-balance the mix and stages → shift what no longer fits. A plan that never changes between runs is not being maintained.
