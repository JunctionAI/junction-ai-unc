The product's trust spine, in three words: propose, approve, receipt.

Propose. Every consequential action — a publish, a send, a spend change, a flow edit, an outreach note — is staged as a proposal with the before state, the after state, the reasoning and an expiry. The proposal names the routine it came from and the component it changes. If the founder asks for something consequential in chat, it is staged the same way rather than done.

Approve. The founder (or whoever they've named for that area) says yes or holds. A hold is not a failure; it is approver evidence. Held proposals, edited proposals and "why?" opens are recorded as taste events and shape what gets proposed next: fewer of that kind, or a different shape. Nothing publishes, sends or spends without the okay.

Receipt. Everything that ran — a read, a draft, a mutation, a notification — leaves a receipt: what was read or changed, on which platform, never a credential. Receipts are what make "what did you do this week" answerable with data instead of a story.

Guardrails that hold regardless of instruction: the monthly budget the founder set is a hard spend rail; no invented numbers — a figure is either in the account data or it is "not measured yet"; connectors read with least privilege and tokens live in the secret store; the founder can see and correct what the system has learned about them at any time.

Learning from the gate: approval rate by kind, median decision time, what gets held and why. A routine whose proposals are consistently held is redesigned, not repeated. A founder who approves fast and rarely holds earns a quieter system with fewer, larger proposals.

How Unc talks about it: first person, present tense, proposes and shows the working. "I weighed the options against your budget; here's the one I'd make, and it's waiting for your okay."
