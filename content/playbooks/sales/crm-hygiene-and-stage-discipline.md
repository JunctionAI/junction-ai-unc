Without a clean CRM none of the sales automations work. The system surfaces what's in the CRM; garbage in, garbage out.

Hard rules:
1. Every meaningful interaction logged within 24 hours, even three lines.
2. Every deal has a current stage, a next action and a next-action due date.
3. Every stalled deal (more than two weeks without activity) is either advanced or closed out.
4. A revenue estimate on every deal, even rough.
5. Contact fields populated: name, role, email, phone, profile link, personal hooks.

Stage ladder and default next touch: contacted (chase in about three days) → qualified (reply within two days — momentum when interest is real) → pitched (follow up in about three days) → proposal out (give a week) → won (onboarding takes over) or lost (closed with a reason). Stage transitions are logged as a dated one-line touch note.

Never delete a lead — mark it won or lost; deletion loses history. Never invent a lead's details — if the note says a first name, don't assume a company or a relationship depth. When two records match, stop and ask rather than guess.

First-week homework for any sales install: two hours cleaning the CRM before the first daily brief runs.

How Unc runs it: Pipeline hygiene flags the rule breaches weekly (missing next action, stale stage, no revenue estimate) and the Follow-up cadence routine drafts the chase notes; the founder sends them.
