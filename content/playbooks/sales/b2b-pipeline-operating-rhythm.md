Most relationship-led salespeople run on memory, inbox and gut, which works to about twenty active relationships. Past that, deals stall because a follow-up slipped on a Tuesday. The system removes the admin that kills the relationship work; it does not replace the relationship.

Five workflows, in the order to install them:
1. Daily pipeline brief (five minutes to read): today's calls with context, deals needing follow-up today (stalled more than a week, a promised-by date hit, a missed meeting), proposals due, wins to acknowledge and losses to learn from, the top three priorities.
2. Stuck-deal surfacer (weekly): every deal with no activity in 7, 14 and 30 days by stage, a likely reason from the thread, a recommended next action with a drafted note, and a decision — advance, revive, or close it out. Stalled deals kept "in the pipeline" for comfort are the habit to kill.
3. Meeting prep brief (two hours before): company summary and recent news, previous interactions, what was promised last time, likely objections with answers, three questions that advance the deal, the desired outcome.
4. Proposal or quote generator: most proposals are 80% reuse; draft the 80% from the template and past similar deals, flag unusual asks, the human customises the 20%.
5. Quarterly account planning for the top accounts: health score, expansion opportunities, risk signals, a 90-day plan with three plays.

Relationship types get different treatment: long-cycle enterprise (depth over activity; quarterly executive reviews), ongoing distribution accounts (monthly performance reviews and proactive alerts), new prospecting (research, sequence drafting, qualification calls, weekly review).

Everything is supervised: drafts come back for the founder's okay; nothing is sent by the system.
