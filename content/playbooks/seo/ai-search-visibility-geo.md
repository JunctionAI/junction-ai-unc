AI search (assistant answers and overviews) is becoming a buying journey. Visibility in it is an operating system, not a tactic:

1. Prompt and journey research replaces keyword research. Map the buying prompts per engine and per market — commercial journeys first, volume vanity never.
2. Diagnose current visibility per engine for those prompts.
3. Source analysis before content creation: for each target prompt, which domains do the engines cite? Those are the placement targets — digital PR into cited sources beats publishing and praying.
4. Implement: content structured for citation (clear question-shaped headings, data and specific claims, an llms.txt file), structured data, feed/product-page/schema consistency, entity consistency, and PR into the cited sources.
5. Validate on a recurring cadence — AI answers are volatile; a one-off audit is worthless.

Measure on three separated layers, never blended: Presence (cited or mentioned), Readiness (content and site consumable by engines), Business impact (revenue moved). A mention with no revenue is a Presence win and a Business null — say both. Optimise for being chosen, not merely cited.

Ecommerce specifics: three-way consistency between feed, product page and structured data; attributes beyond the minimum with normalised units; agent-ready journeys.

Failure modes: counting citations as revenue; optimising content while the feed and product pages disagree (an invisible ceiling no content fixes); chasing every engine equally instead of the one or two where the buyers actually ask.

How Unc runs it: the AI search visibility routine keeps a named prompt set per market, re-checks it on cadence, and reports the three layers separately, including zeros.
