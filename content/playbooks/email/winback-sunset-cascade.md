Unengaged profiles drag inbox placement for the buyers who matter, so the list needs a cascade — but the order is the whole method. Winback first, sunset second, suppress last. Never sunset a valid long-cycle buyer because they haven't opened in 90 days.

Winback (lapsed 60–90+ days since purchase):
1. "We've missed seeing you around" — acknowledge the absence without blame, no discount.
2. About a week later — what's new: new arrivals, best-sellers, a real reason to return.
3. About two weeks later — the offer, and this is the first place a discount belongs.
Give an easy preference-update path in every email so people can lower frequency instead of leaving.

Sunset (true multi-signal disengagement — no open or click in 90 days AND no site activity in 90 days AND no purchase in 120 days):
1. "Are you still interested?" — one clear yes button.
2. About a week later — "Last chance", then suppress the non-responders.

Suppression is a deliverability act, not a loss: a clean list outperforms a large list. Expect a small share to re-engage; the rest were costing reputation.

Reporting on this must be honest: winback reactivation rates are usually low single digits. Treat every reactivation as the win it is, and treat the suppressed count as list health, not churn.
