Email is a relationship, not a broadcast. You earn the inbox by being useful, then you monetise a warm, segmented list.

Before any email ships, three questions: why should I open, why should I read, why should I click. If any is unanswered the email isn't ready.

Subject line: about half the outcome. Two variants from two different angles (curiosity vs direct, question vs mechanism), under about ten words, mobile-legible, curiosity plus clarity so it isn't vague. Pick the winner on opens-to-clicks, not opens alone. Keep a no-"free" variant for broad segments — spam-trigger words cost deliverability on cold lists.

Preview text: a genuine second subject line that extends the promise, under about 90 characters. Never let it auto-pull the first body line.

Body: inverted pyramid — the most important thing first, then the supporting detail framed as benefit, then the single call to action. Feature to benefit flip: not "our X has this feature" but "this outcome, thanks to this feature". Second person, plain, sounds like a friend. Get to the point.

Lifecycle-matched message: welcome = warm intro plus the reciprocity gift; post-purchase = reassurance and care tips before any cross-sell; winback = acknowledge the absence without blame and give a real reason to return; VIP = exclusivity and genuine thanks.

Two competing calls to action both lose. One primary; a secondary must be visibly subordinate.

Then run the copy taste gate last: voice, craft, slop — the copy sounds like the founder, every claim is specific and falsifiable, no AI tells.
