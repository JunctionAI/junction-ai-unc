Analysis is not reporting. Reporting says what the numbers were; analysis says what they mean, what's broken, and what to test next. If an analysis doesn't produce a learning that changes behaviour, it was a waste of time.

Every channel, top-down:
1. Business impact — does this channel contribute to revenue, profit and growth? Attributed revenue (store-verified, not platform-claimed), share of total, return, trend.
2. Channel health — is it structurally sound? Costs trending (CPM, CPA), frequency, list growth and engaged share, indexation, deliverability, audience saturation.
3. Execution performance — are the campaigns, flows and content working? Top and bottom performers, win rates, creative concepts (not just ads), segments, subject-line patterns.
4. Insight extraction — what did we learn we didn't know? Learnings, hypotheses, next-cycle actions.

Never skip levels. A channel can have great execution metrics while contributing nothing to business impact; the hierarchy prevents false confidence.

Then the cross-channel view: attribution overlap (two platforms claiming the same order), true blended return, which channel opens and which closes, whether email re-engages paid-acquired customers, where the next dollar produces the most, which channel has headroom.

Cadence: weekly per active channel (30–60 minutes: pull, compare, top/bottom three, anomalies, one to three learnings, two or three actions); monthly all four levels plus cross-channel; quarterly the strategic review and playbook audit.

The biggest return from analysis is often what to stop doing, not what to start.
